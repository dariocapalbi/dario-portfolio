Fitmate

← Back to work

UX/UI Design · Mobile App · Wellness · 2023

Let's crush our fitness goals together.

Fitness shouldn't feel like another task on your to-do list. Finding the motivation to exercise is easy on Monday morning. Keeping it going is another story.

Fitmate is a wellness app concept designed to make fitness more motivating, personal and social — bringing workouts, personal trainers and fitness meetups into one experience.

The idea was simple: what if staying active didn't have to mean doing it alone?

The problem wasn't knowing what to do

It was actually doing it.

I started by looking at existing fitness apps such as Ejercicios en Casa, Adidas Training and MyFitnessPal. Most of them already did a good job at providing workouts, tracking activity and helping users set goals. But something felt missing.

Fitness isn't only about having access to the right workout. It's also about motivation, accountability and finding something that fits into your actual life. That became the opportunity for Fitmate.

So I started listening

I used surveys and six user interviews to understand how people really approached fitness in their everyday lives. A few patterns quickly became clear.

People struggled to find time. They found it difficult to stay motivated. They wanted more personalised guidance. And many felt that exercising with other people could make the experience more enjoyable — and easier to stick with.

So the challenge wasn't simply how do we help people exercise? It was:

How do we help people keep going?

Meet Emma

30 · Recruiter · London

Emma has a busy professional life and wants to become more consistent with fitness. She doesn't necessarily need another fitness app telling her what to do — she needs something that can adapt to her lifestyle. She wants flexibility, guidance and motivation, without feeling that exercise is taking over her life.

What gets in her way: finding time, staying motivated, choosing the right workout, knowing how to perform exercises correctly, and balancing fitness with work and her social life.

From Monday motivation to a sustainable routine

Mapping Emma's journey helped me see where the experience was breaking down. She could start with good intentions, but then everyday life got in the way.

A busy schedule made workouts harder to maintain. A lack of accountability made motivation disappear. Too many choices made it difficult to know where to start.

This led to one of the most important ideas behind Fitmate:

Fitness is easier to maintain when you don't have to do it alone.

What if your fitness app felt more like a community?

This became the foundation of the product. Instead of building another app focused purely on tracking calories, steps or workouts, I wanted Fitmate to connect three things.

  1. 01 Move Find workouts that fit your goals.
  2. 02 Connect Meet people through fitness meetups.
  3. 03 Get support Find personal trainers who can provide guidance.

Everything else came after that.

Four simple ways to keep moving

The experience was organised around four main areas.

Meetup
Discover fitness activities and join other people.
Workout
Find and follow workouts based on your needs.
Personal Trainer
Get more personalised support and guidance.
Account
Keep track of your activity and manage your profile.

The goal was to keep the experience simple enough to understand immediately, while still giving users room to explore.

Before making it pretty, I made it messy

I explored different ideas using Crazy 8s, sketching multiple solutions quickly before deciding what was worth developing. From there I moved into low- and mid-fidelity wireframes.

This let me focus on the experience first. Where should things go? What does the user need first? What can be removed? What should happen next?

Only once the structure started to make sense did I move into the visual design.

Making fitness feel energetic

I wanted Fitmate to feel positive and approachable rather than clinical or overly serious. The visual direction was built around bold, energetic colours, with purple and pink as the main colours and blue, yellow and magenta as accents.

For typography I chose Urbanist, for its clean and contemporary character. Custom icons and illustrations gave the interface a more distinctive personality.

The idea was to make opening the app feel less like opening a productivity tool — and more like joining something you actually want to be part of.

And then it started to feel real

The final high-fidelity prototype brought the different parts of the experience together. Users could discover workouts, explore fitness meetups, find personal trainers and keep track of their activity inside the same ecosystem.

The interface keeps the most relevant actions close at hand while making the community side visible throughout.

But the first version is never the final version

After building the prototype in Figma I collected feedback and identified what could go further. The next iteration could include:

  • More workout routines
  • Video and detailed exercise instructions
  • More complete trainer profiles
  • Weekly and monthly activity statistics
  • Improved account settings
  • A dedicated news and content section

These became the starting point for the product roadmap.

What stayed with me

Fitmate taught me that designing a fitness product isn't really about designing workouts. It's about understanding why someone starts, why they stop, and what might make them come back.

The project pushed me to look past features and think about the emotional side of an experience — motivation, confidence, accountability, connection.

Because sometimes the best way to help someone reach a goal isn't to give them more information.

It's to make them want to keep going.
